# Sleeping Bag Temperature Ratings

The temperature printed on a sleeping bag is not the temperature at which you will be warm. Here is how to read it.

## Comfort, limit and extreme

Most bags list three numbers from a standard lab test. The comfort rating is the temperature at which a cold-sleeping person, lying relaxed, should sleep comfortably through the night. The limit rating is the lowest temperature at which a warm-sleeping person can sleep curled up without getting cold. The extreme rating is a survival figure only; you would be dangerously cold, and it should never be used for planning.

## Choosing a rating

Choose a bag whose comfort rating is at or below the coldest night you expect. If you sleep cold, or camp at altitude, pick a bag rated 10 degrees Fahrenheit colder than your coldest expected night. It is easy to vent a warm bag and impossible to warm up a cold one.

## Down and synthetic

Down is lighter and packs smaller, but loses warmth when wet. Synthetic insulation stays warm when damp and costs less, but weighs more.

## Care and storage

Store your bag loose in a large cotton or mesh sack, not compressed in its stuff sack, which flattens the insulation over time. Air it out after every trip and wash it only when needed, using a cleaner made for sleeping bags.
