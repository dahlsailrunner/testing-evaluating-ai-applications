# Layering Guide for Cold and Changing Weather

Layers let you adjust to the weather and to how hard you are working. Think of three layers, each with a job.

## Base layer

The base layer sits against your skin and moves sweat away from it. Choose merino wool or a synthetic fabric. Avoid cotton: it holds moisture and leaves you cold once you stop moving.

## Mid layer

The mid layer traps warm air. Fleece is breathable and dries quickly; a light insulated jacket packs smaller. Take a mid layer on almost every outing, even in summer in the mountains.

## Outer layer

The outer layer, or shell, blocks wind and rain. Look for a waterproof, breathable jacket with a hood and underarm vents. In dry, cold weather a wind-resistant softshell may be enough.

## Managing temperature

Start a hike slightly cool, because you will warm up within ten minutes. Remove a layer before you start to sweat, and add one at rest stops before you get cold. Adjusting early is easier than recovering from being too hot or too cold.

## Hands, head and feet

Carry a warm hat and gloves in every season but summer. Wool or synthetic socks keep feet warm even when damp.
