# Kayak Sizing and Transport

## Choosing a length

Shorter kayaks, around 9 to 10 feet, turn easily and are good for calm lakes and slow rivers. Touring kayaks of 12 to 14 feet track straighter and glide farther with each stroke, making them better for open water and day trips. Longer boats are faster but harder to turn and to carry.

## Weight capacity and fit

Pick a kayak whose weight capacity is at least 50 pounds above your combined body weight and gear. A kayak loaded close to its limit sits low and handles poorly. Check that the cockpit fits you: your knees should rest lightly under the deck and your feet reach the footbraces with a slight bend in the legs.

## Stability

Wider kayaks feel more stable at rest and suit beginners and anglers. Narrower kayaks feel tippy at first but paddle faster and more efficiently.

## Transporting a kayak

Carry a kayak on roof-rack crossbars using foam blocks or padded cradles. Strap it down with two cam straps over the hull, and add bow and stern tie-down lines to the front and rear of your vehicle to stop it lifting in wind. Do not overtighten straps, which can dent the hull.

## Storage

Store a kayak indoors or under cover, supported on its side or in padded cradles, never resting on its keel. Prolonged sun exposure weakens the plastic.
